Absen Saturn SA2.6 tiles ×112 verified

The sheet calls for two 13'×23' hanging screens. 112 ÷ 2 = 56 tiles per screen = 7 wide × 8 tall = 23' × 13'. The math checks.

Tile: 1000×500×70 mm, 9.5 kg (21 lb) · pixel pitch 2.6 mm · 384×192 px per tile · 1500 nits
Per screen: 2688×1536 px · about 532 kg (1,170 lb) of tiles alone
Power: 510 W/m² max, 270 W/m² average = about 255 W max / 135 W avg per tile, so roughly 14 kW max and 7.6 kW avg per screen (28.6 kW max for both)

Handle by the frame, never touch the LED face, carry flat. Lock every latch before it flies.

Chauvet Ovation E-910FC ×12 verified

Full-colour LED ellipsoidal. Works like a leko: four framing shutters and a lens tube shape the beam.

Light engine: RGBAL (red, green, blue, amber, lime) LEDs · 10 colour-temperature presets, 2800–6500 K
Power: about 220 W, 1.79 A at 120 V · max 6 per circuit at 120 V (never over 12 A), so 12 fixtures need at least 2 circuits
Weight: 15.4 lb body; 19.8 lb with a 26° tube
DMX modes: 1, 3, 5, 7, 10, 12, 13 and 16 channels, plus HSV (3). Address 001–512, highest start address depends on the mode
Overhead: mount with a clamp and a safety cable; keep 20″ (50 cm) clear of surfaces

Fan modes: Auto, On, Off, Silent. Off and Silent reduce output and run the body hotter. Soft-focus filter: don't use it with gobos or big shutter cuts — it can heat-damage. A display reading −40°C or 125°C is a temperature-sensor fault: stop using it and report it.

The sheet pairs these with the Ovations. Chauvet Maverick MK1 Hybrid ×4 verified

A moving head with CMY colour mixing, two prisms, gobos and zoom. It runs on a discharge lamp, not LEDs. These are the flown movers the award-winner spot special comes from.

Lamp: 440 W Osram Sirius
Power: 670 W, 5.45 A at 120 V each — four is about 22 A, so they can't all share one 20 A circuit
Weight: 66.7 lb (30.3 kg) — this is a two-person lift
DMX: 27 or 30 channels · Pan/tilt: 540°/270°
Mount: Omega bracket included; clamps and a safety cable are sold separately — every flown unit needs a safety cable

Chauvet WELL Panel ×6 verified

Battery-powered, wireless LED wash panel. It receives control over W-DMX (not D-Fi).

Runtime: selectable modes of 3, 5, 8 or 12 hours (the shorter modes trade brightness for runtime)
Charging: about 4 hours with the supplied charger, with the panel powered off; it can run while plugged in
Rating: IP65 · 22.6 lb (10.3 kg) · Omega bracket, clamps separate, safety cable overhead
Range: up to about 300 m from the W-DMX transmitter — people and water can block it
Address: 001–509 depending on mode (4, 8, 12, 13 or 21 channel)

Pairing: set the DMX start address, then Wireless Setting → Operating Mode → Receive, W-DMX On, and press the transmitter's reset. To re-pair: Wireless Setting → Receive Reset → Yes, then hold RESET on the transmitter until its signal light is solid. A “?” before the signal bars means it's waiting for a connection.

Wireless — Shure ULXD (G50)

ULXD system ×6 verified

Six receivers (ULXD4), six bodypacks (ULXD1), six handhelds (ULXD2, SM58 capsule) and six WL185 lavs.

Band G50: 470–534 MHz · Batteries: Shure SB900 rechargeable or 2 AA · about 11+ hours on an SB900, about 9 hours on alkaline AA at 10 mW
Receiver RF lights: blue = good link, red = interference, off = no transmitter. No blue light means no audio
RF strength lights: amber = normal, red = overload
Audio lights: green = signal present, yellow = normal peaks, red = overload/limiter

Mute: a muted transmitter's light turns red and the receiver says “Tx Muted”. A muted receiver channel says “Rx MUTED”.
Locked buttons: the transmitter or receiver menu can be locked; unlock it from the utility menu.
IR sync (matches a transmitter to its receiver): turn on the transmitter, press the receiver's sync button, line up the IR windows until the receiver's IR port lights red, and wait for the success message.
Battery type: if you use AA, set the transmitter's battery type to AA so the time readout is right.

Comms

Analogy: wired comms is a conference call where everyone shares one phone line. FreeSpeak is the same call, cordless.

Clear-Com FreeSpeak II ×2 systems verified

Digital wireless comms (DECT). The sheet says “5 Channel System” — that may mean five beltpacks per antenna; check.

Capacity: a base station takes up to 20 beltpacks; each antenna handles up to 5 (4 is safer in a noisy room)
Antenna cable: shielded Cat5/5e/6, up to 1,000 m (24 AWG); it powers the antenna up to about 300 m
Antenna lights: green + amber on = powered · amber flashing = data link · amber solid = synced and ready
Coverage check: signal (RSSI) of about 30 or higher; below that a beltpack may lose audio

Register a beltpack:
1. On the base: Main Menu → System → Reg → Start (it times out after 2 minutes).
2. On the beltpack: hold the menu key 2 seconds, scroll to System Connect, press D.
3. Find the system showing V and P. If it doesn't show, restart registration on the base.
4. Enter the 4-digit code (default 0000; the base shows it at Menu → System → Reg → PIN).
5. Pick an open role. Done.

Some FreeSpeak II beltpacks are 1.9 GHz and some 2.4 GHz — check the label and don't mix them.

Rig / Power

Power

Motion Labs 3-phase, 200A, 9-hole ×1

The main distro: feeder in, circuits out. What's on the nine outlets isn't on the sheet — check before you plug in. Power trunk ×1 holds the rest of the power gear.

Edison runs see Cables

Edison and tri-taps feed the breakout rooms. Quick math: watts ÷ volts = amps. A 20A circuit at 120V holds about 2400W, but stay at 80% for anything that runs all day.

Cam-lok — the only two rules.
Hook-up: GROUND (green) first, NEUTRAL (white) second, then the hot legs in the order they sit on that distro, working away from ground.
Strike: hot legs first, then neutral, GROUND LAST.
Never teach a fixed phase order (black/red/blue). The panel layout sets it. Ground is usually on the left, but some panels have it on the right.
Ask first: confirm with the lead that the feeder is de-energized before you connect or disconnect cam-lok. Know the sequence, but only do it when it's your call.

Cable habits

Over-under coil

Alternating loops lie flat and throw out straight. Coiling one way twists the conductors.

Ramp or tape across walkways

Anything crossing a public path gets covered. No loose loops in the middle of an aisle.

Label both ends

A labeled cable gets found in seconds. An unlabeled one costs everyone.
